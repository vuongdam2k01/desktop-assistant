### Session 2026-09-12 (drafting decisions, first five)

The decision-maker was not available for an interview, and each question below is answered here by the drafting
agent from the spike measurements and the constitution rather than from preference. Every answer is recorded with
the evidence that settles it so the decision-maker can overturn one by reading a single line. Where an answer
could not be derived that way it is not answered here; it is in `## Open`.

- Q: Should the manifest schema and the adapter interface be frozen now, at two connectors, or left informal until a third platform has been written? → A: Freeze now as contracts at version 1.0.0, with additive extension defined as MINOR. The zero-core-change property was measured, not assumed — VERIFIED, `spikes/SP-19-connector-framework/REPORT.md#0-ket-luan` — and it is a property of the boundary rather than of the two connectors; an informal boundary is one the third connector renegotiates and the core then absorbs, which is what principle VI forbids. The cost of freezing early is a MINOR bump per missing field, and `evolution.md` makes that cost explicit. (Patched: `contracts/connector-manifest.md`, `contracts/connector-adapter.md`, `evolution.md`.)
- Q: When a manifest fails validation, does the product load the tools that are valid or refuse the connector entirely? → A: Refuse the whole connector and load no tool from it. A partially loaded manifest offers operations whose reversibility nobody declared, which principle IV forbids outright; and the failure belongs where the connector author sees it rather than where a user meets it. (Patched: `specs/connector/spec.md` — "A manifest is loaded whole or not at all".)
- Q: Where do the standard connector error codes live — in the manifest schema, in the adapter interface, or in a contract of their own? → A: In the adapter contract, which is the surface that produces them, referenced from the manifest rather than repeated in it. They are a `closed` variability point: a connector may not invent a code, because the job manager decides what to retry and what ends a job from that fixed set — VERIFIED for the three states that end a job cleanly, `spikes/SP-19-connector-framework/REPORT.md#3-dau-vao-cho-tai-lieu-ky-thuat`. (Patched: `contracts/connector-adapter.md` Error Matrix, `model.md` Variability.)
- Q: Who checks and refreshes an authorisation that is about to expire — the adapter at the moment of the call, or the job manager before the job starts? → A: The job manager checks before it starts and refreshes when less validity remains than the configured margin; the adapter still refreshes on demand, because a long job can outlive any margin. The spike measured the cost of an in-sequence refresh at roughly 300 ms and recommended the pre-flight check — `spikes/SP-19-connector-framework/REPORT.md#4-rui-ro-moi-phat-hien`. The margin's default of five minutes is that recommendation and not a measurement, and `verification.md` records it as such. (Patched: `specs/job/spec.md` — "A job establishes its connectors' authorisation before it starts".)
- Q: What happens when the user disconnects a platform that publishes no revocation endpoint? → A: The disconnection proceeds, erases the authorisation everywhere it is held for the account, and states plainly that the platform itself still lists the integration, with a direct route to the page where the user can withdraw it. The manifest declares the absence rather than leaving it to be discovered: Notion publishes no such endpoint while Google does — VERIFIED, `spikes/SP-19-connector-framework/REPORT.md#4-rui-ro-moi-phat-hien`. Refusing to disconnect was rejected because it strands the user with an authorisation they cannot remove from either side; silently reporting success was rejected because it tells the user something untrue about their own account. (Patched: `specs/connector/spec.md` — MODIFIED "Disconnecting revokes and erases the authorisation".)

### Session 2026-09-12 (drafting decisions, remaining three)

- Q: Is the release channel's scope profile a build-time choice, a user setting, or a property recorded with each authorisation? → A: It is selected by the release channel's configuration and recorded with the authorisation it was granted under, so a later change of profile is a re-consent rather than a silent widening or narrowing of what the user agreed to. Profile switching costs no manifest and no adapter change — VERIFIED, `spikes/SP-19-connector-framework/REPORT.md#1-tra-loi-tung-cau-hoi` (Q8). (Patched: `specs/connector/spec.md` — MODIFIED "Only the scope of an enabled capability is requested".)
- Q: Does this change accept connectors the product did not write — third-party manifests or tools imported from an external protocol server? → A: No, and the slot is reserved rather than left undecided. Every manifest in this scope ships with the build and is first-party, which is why the adapter may run in the process that owns the ledger and the gate. Importing an external tool description was measured as a conversion of no architectural distance — VERIFIED, `spikes/SP-19-connector-framework/REPORT.md#1-tra-loi-tung-cau-hoi` (Q11) — so what remains is not a technical gap but the isolation, review and trust decision that accepting other people's code requires. `model.md` records it as a reserved point with its phase, rationale and activation condition, as the constitution's Reserved By Design section requires. (Patched: `model.md` Variability, `evolution.md` Reserved Slots.)
- Q: Does a connector adapter run in the process that owns the ledger and the approval gate, or behind an isolation boundary of its own? → A: In the same process, for first-party manifests only. The adapter holds the authorisation it must present to the platform, and moving it across a boundary would either move the credential with it or add a second credential-bearing channel — both worse than the risk the boundary would remove, while every manifest is first-party. The condition that changes this answer is the reserved point above, and it is stated there rather than left implicit. (Patched: `design.md` Execution Boundary & Protocol Topology, D5.)

## Assumptions

- Two connectors of deliberately opposite shape are enough to freeze the boundary; the third is expected to extend the schema additively rather than to break it. Recorded in `proposal.md` and revisited by the extension procedure in `evolution.md`.
- The sanitization list a document- or database-shaped connector must declare is the six computed fields identified in `req-003-notion-compensation`; other platform families may need a different list, and the obligation is therefore stated as "declare what must be excluded" rather than as that list.
- The reconciliation declaration each tool carries is `job/contracts/tool-reconciliation@0.1.0` unchanged. This change requires it in the manifest and does not redefine it.
- No application code exists yet, so nothing here is a migration of a running product; the only migration is from the drafted `connector-manifest@0.1.0` to the frozen `1.0.0`, and `evolution.md` states it.
